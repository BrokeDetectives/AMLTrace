Ablation - what each layer adds

Layers are switched on cumulatively. The final row is unsupervised ML with no graph context at all, which is the honest baseline this project argues against.

Hard negatives - lawful business shaped like crime

Precision on a ledger where only criminals form triangles is close to meaningless. The ledger therefore contains families of entirely legitimate accounts engineered to reproduce each typology's topology. None of them is labelled fraud. Anything caught here is the detector matching shape without understanding purpose.

Discriminator ablation - what stops the look-alikes

The detection layers find the shapes; four economic tests decide whether a shape is a crime. Each is switched off in isolation below. The final row is what a topology-only AML detector actually scores.

Robustness - threshold sensitivity

Every threshold perturbed independently; Jaccard similarity of the resulting alert set against the baseline. 1.00 means the same accounts were flagged.

Adversarial test

Every threshold in layers 1 to 4 is published in this interface, and a number a launderer can read is a number they can sit just outside. These two ledgers were built to do exactly that. Both are scored twice: with the adaptive layer switched off, and with it on.

⤓ JSON

What a launderer who has read the rulebook does

The relay ring places every parameter outside a published threshold at once: hops five days apart so the circular-flow window never closes, amounts far below the reporting band, and reciprocal cover traffic on every member so the dedication ratio stays low. The peeling chain is a typology this engine has no layer for at all, included to test whether the adaptive layer generalises or has simply been fitted to the first ledger.

How the adaptive layer works

Three properties that follow from moving other people's money through other people's accounts. None of them is a threshold anyone can step around.

Where it still fails

Stated here rather than left for a reviewer to find.

  • Two of the three tests must agree before anything is flagged, so a launderer who randomises timing and skims heavily and moves money through genuinely busy trading accounts can still get under it. The cost of doing all three is that the money moves slower and leaks more, which is a deterrent rather than a detection.
  • The layer needs a relay of at least three hops. A single mule paid once and abandoned leaves nothing to trace.
  • Centrality and the anomaly model still change no headline metric on this ledger. They earn their place in triage, deciding which alert an analyst opens first, not in detection. That distinction is measured on the model evidence page.

Alert economics

Detection quality is not the decision a bank makes. The decision is how many analysts to staff, and every threshold in this system is really a budget line. This converts the current configuration into hours, headcount and rupees.
